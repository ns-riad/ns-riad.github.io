  1. IV
    String Stability Analysis of Followerstopper for Mixed-Autonomy Vehicle Platoons
    Md Nazmus Shakib, Shihab Uddin, and Rahul Bhadani
    In 2026 IEEE Intelligent Vehicles Symposium (IV), 2026
    Abs Bib

    This study evaluates the string stability of the Followerstopper controller, a known wave-dampening adaptive cruise control algorithm, in an eight-vehicle platoon scenario. An eight-vehicle platoon (one human-driven leader and seven automated followers) is simulated using a real-world leader speed profile, implemented in both a microscopic traffic simulator SUMO and a high-fidelity physics-engine simulator CARLA. We employ a time-domain evaluation framework with metrics such as head-to-tail velocity amplification, L2-norm disturbance attenuation, relative speed differences, and spacing error. Results show that the Followerstopper controller nearly eliminates disturbance amplification: the head-to-tail amplification factor is approximately 0.994 in both SUMO and CARLA, indicating slight attenuation of the leader’s perturbations. The disturbance "energy" (quantified via the L2 norm of speed errors) decreases downstream, and once the Followerstopper is activated, large relative speed oscillations and spacing deviations are rapidly damped. These improvements are consistent across both simulators, which demonstrates robust performance of Followerstopper under realistic vehicle dynamics. The proposed time-domain-based string stability assessment in realistic mixed-autonomy settings indicates that Followerstopper effectively stabilizes traffic flow by dissipating disturbances in platoon formation.

  1. ITSC
    Modeling Electric Vehicle Car-Following Behavior: Classical vs Machine Learning Approach
    Shihab Uddin, Md Nazmus Shakib, and Rahul Bhadani
    In Proceedings of the IEEE Intelligent Transportation Systems Conference (ITSC), 2025
    Abs Bib HTML

    The increasing adoption of electric vehicles (EVs) necessitates understanding their driving behavior to enhance traffic safety and develop smart driving systems. This study compares classical and machine learning models for EV car-following behavior. Classical models include the Intelligent Driver Model (IDM), Optimum Velocity Model (OVM), Optimal Velocity Relative Velocity (OVRV), and a simplified CACC model, while the machine learning approach employs a Random Forest Regressor. Using a real-world dataset of an EV following an internal combustion engine (ICE) vehicle under varied driving conditions, we calibrated classical model parameters by minimizing the RMSE between predictions and real data. The Random Forest model predicts acceleration using spacing, speed, and gap type as inputs. Results demonstrate the Random Forest’s superior accuracy, achieving RMSEs of 0.0046 (medium gap), 0.0016 (long gap), and 0.0025 (extra-long gap). Among physics-based models, CACC performed best, with an RMSE of 2.67 for long gaps. These findings highlight the machine learning model’s outperformance across all scenarios. Such models are valuable for simulating EV behavior and analyzing mixed-autonomy traffic dynamics in EV-integrated environments.

  2. INFORMS
    Learning-Based CPU Burst Prediction for Efficient Scheduling in Operating Systems
    Md Nazmus Shakib and Rahul Bhadani
    In INFORMS Annual Meeting, 2025
    Poster accepted
    Abs Bib HTML

    In high-performance computing environments, scheduling decisions rely heavily on estimating how long a process will occupy the CPU. Conventional estimation techniques often fall short under varying workloads, limiting their effectiveness in modern, multi-user systems. This work introduces a data-driven framework that uses machine learning to predict process burst times using only features known at submission time. We evaluate several machine learning models, including decision trees, random forests, and multi-layer perceptrons, using real-world scheduling traces. Among them, the decision tree model offers the best trade-off between accuracy and speed. It achieves a mean absolute error (MAE) of 3514.09 and relative absolute error (RAE) of 12.64%, which is significantly lower than the exponential averaging baseline (MAE: 14833 ms, RAE: 53.34%), while keeping inference time per sample around 0.0033 milliseconds, making it suitable for real-time scheduling. Compared to exponential averaging, the learning-based models provide improved accuracy across a variety of workload conditions. The early availability of accurate predictions allows schedulers to make better decisions without introducing significant delay. This helps reduce wait times and improves overall CPU utilization. The study also includes model tuning and performance validation using 95% confidence intervals to ensure the results are consistent and reliable. By introducing a fast and accurate prediction method, this work supports efficient CPU scheduling strategies that respond well to real-world system demands.

  1. IEEE
    Analyzing Aggressive Driving Patterns with GPS Data: A Machine Learning Approach
    Md. Ariful Islam, MD Mijanur Rahman, Md. Orasur Rahman, and 1 more author
    In 2024 6th International Conference on Sustainable Technologies for Industry 5.0 (STI), 2024
    Abs Bib HTML

    Aggressive driving behavior is a critical issue with significant implications for road safety and traffic management. Researchers have extensively studied methods to predict such behaviors, utilizing various data sources and machine learning techniques. This research focuses on predicting aggressive driving behavior using GPS data from the Green University Bus Route Dataset. We developed a novel preprocessing algorithm, along with label encoding and standardization techniques, to prepare the dataset for analysis. Seven machine-learning algorithms were used to guess how often aggressive driving would happen. These were Decision Tree classifiers (DTC), AdaBoost classifiers (ABC), Gradient Boosting classifiers (GBC), Extra Tree classifiers (ETC), Support Vector classifiers (SVC), Gaussian Naive Bayes (GNB), and Random Forest classifiers (RFC). Our results indicate that the GBC, ETC, SVC, and RFC outperform other models, with each one achieving the highest accuracy of 99.445%. This study demonstrates the potential of GPS data to effectively predict aggressive driving behavior, offering valuable insights for improving road safety measures.

  1. IEEE
    Enhanced Road Lane Marking Detection System: A CNN-Based Approach for Safe Driving
    Md. Romzan Alom, Tarqul Alam Opi, Hasanul Islam Palok, and 3 more authors
    In 2023 5th International Conference on Sustainable Technologies for Industry 5.0 (STI), 2023
    Abs Bib HTML

    Accurate detection and interpretation of road lane markings (RLM) are essential for maintaining safe and efficient traffic flow. However, conventional computer vision methods encounter significant difficulties in handling occluded images. This research presents an innovative system named Road Lane Markings Detection (RLMD) that employs deep learning techniques to predict RLM from occluded images and provides their corresponding meanings. To address the limitations of existing approaches, a comprehensive dataset of RLM images, covering diverse occlusion scenarios, is created. Several deep learning baseline network architectures are used and trained on this dataset, enabling precise RLM prediction. Moreover, algorithms are devised to extract the meanings associated with various types of RLM, including broken lines, solid lines, and double solid lines in both yellow and white colors. Experimental evaluations demonstrate the system’s effectiveness in real-world scenarios, showcasing its potential to enhance road safety and support driver assistance systems with 96.39% using convolutional neural networks (CNNs) layers. By overcoming the challenges posed by occlusions, the proposed system contributes significantly to the field of RLM prediction and its corresponding meanings using deep learning.

  2. IEEE
    Enhanced Hamming Codes: Reducing Redundant Bit for Efficient Error Detection and Correction
    Md. Romzan Alom, Md Nazmus Shakib, and Muhammad Aminur Rahaman
    In 2023 5th International Conference on Sustainable Technologies for Industry 5.0 (STI), 2023
    Abs Bib HTML

    Error detection and correction are crucial for data transmission and storage systems, and Traditional Hamming codes (THC) may lead to inefficiencies in storage and transmission overhead. This paper presents a novel approach called Enhanced Hamming Codes (EHC), which aims to reduce redundant bits while maintaining error detection and correction capabilities. The study reviews the existing methods and proposes an enhanced encoding scheme that minimizes redundant bits. The enhanced encoding scheme is a storage-utilized system where a suitable base is extracted from the data. The methodology utilizes optimized error detection algorithms and optimized encoding techniques for efficient resource utilization. Experimental evaluations show that the proposed approach achieves a successful reduction in redundant bits, leading to enhanced storage usage for overall data transmission. Additionally, the Hamming Code maintains robust error detection and correction capabilities, ensuring reliable data integrity. This research has practical implications in various domains, such as telecommunications, data storage systems, and network protocols. The EHC technique offers a promising solution for reducing redundant bits in error detection and correction codes, demonstrating its effectiveness and efficiency. This research opens avenues for further exploration and optimization of error detection and correction techniques, ultimately leading to more reliable and resource-efficient data transmission and storage systems.

  1. IEEE
    An adaptive system for detecting driving abnormality of individual drivers using Gaussian mixture model
    Md Nazmus Shakib, Md Shamim, Md Nazirul Hasan Shawon, and 3 more authors
    In 2021 5th International Conference on Electrical Engineering and Information Communication Technology (ICEEICT), 2021
    Best Paper Abs Bib HTML

    This paper received the ‘1st prize award‘ in the ‘Best Paper Category‘ out of the 103 accepted papers.

    Abnormal driving of a driver often leads to some undesirable situations. To avoid such circumstances, this paper proposes a driver-adaptive system that can detect abnormality within one’s own driving style. To achieve such a feat, firstly, a soft clustering approach is proposed to determine the driving style in three categories - aggressive, moderate, and protective. Then, it proposes an algorithm to analyze the driving profile and detect the variation of abnormality of the driver. Statistics of different vehicle kinetic characteristics, i.e., velocity, space and time headway, acceleration, jerk factor, etc., are considered for the clustering process. Maximal Information Coefficient (MIC) is used to select the important features. Analyzing the results of different clustering algorithms such as Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM), FCM, and K-means, we used GMM in the proposed system for determining real-time driving style. The actual driving style is determined using the long-term driving history of the subject vehicle. Using GMM’s soft clustering properties, the current deviation is measured by comparing the real-time driving style to the actual style. A learning factor is accommodated to update the actual style in every cycle so that the proposed system can adapt to the driver’s skill-changing state. Real-life driving depends on real-time human decisions. Therefore, we have used the reconstructed NGSIM I-80 real-life traffic data to build and validate the proposed system.

  2. IEEE
    A DNN Based Driving Scheme for Anticipatory Car Following Using Road-Speed Profile
    Most Kaniz Fatema Isha, Md Nazirul Hasan Shawon, Md Shamim, and 3 more authors
    In 2021 IEEE Intelligent Vehicles Symposium (IV), 2021
    Abs Bib HTML

    The state prediction of the preceding vehicle (PV) is essential for anticipatory driving that enables a vehicle to take early steps for efficient driving. Such driving results in energy efficiency and traffic flow improvement but also requires repeated rigorous computations. Here we propose a Deep Neural Network (DNN) based driving scheme (DDS) that is trained to predict the PV and decide the control input of a host vehicle (HV) using two DNN models. We use the Road-Speed Profile (RSP), which provides approximated traffic speeds along the road using information from limited connected vehicles, to predict the future speed of the PV. Next, with the predicted speed of the PV, the control input is instantly determined to drive the HV efficiently. For making the optimal control decisions, the DNN is trained using a Model Predictive Control (MPC) scheme that generates the control input of the HV by minimizing a typical objective function reflecting costs related to inefficient driving. In the simulation, it is found that the proposed DDS guides the HV to change acceleration early in the optimized way by predicting the PV, particularly in changing traffic conditions, which results in a significant reduction in fuel consumption of the HV. Most remarkably, the DDS can determine the decision almost in real-time, which removes the necessity of setting a long step in the discrete-time control framework as found in MPC to match with its computation time. The proposed DDS is evaluated in some extreme cases and compared with traditional driving.
